Side project: learning humanoids and VLA by building one
Kawasemi Humanoid: Building a 3D-Printed Humanoid
as a Platform for VLA and Failure-Aware Embodied AI
An original small humanoid, built step by step from one arm to VLA manipulation and bipedal walking
Masahiro MutoPersonal project (planning and early design)Target: January 2028
MOTIVATION Research on VLA and world models usually runs on off-the-shelf robots, so the joints, gearboxes, power and low-level control stay a black box. Using Berkeley Humanoid Lite as a technical reference, I am building an original small humanoid in the order CAD → 3D printing → ROS 2 → VLA → bipedal walking, and turning it into a real-robot platform for my research.

1What I am building

An original small humanoid made with a Bambu Lab P2S and Autodesk Fusion. It is not a copy of BHL: the body shape and joints are my own design.

ItemTarget (initial)
Height60–90 cm (tentative)
DoF6–7 per arm (12–14 for both), 6–7 per leg
HandsGrippers first, multi-fingered hands later
Frame3D-printed PLA / PETG / ASA + metal reinforcement, TPU soles
DriveBLDC motors + reducers (custom cycloidal or commercial)
ComputeRaspberry Pi 5 / Jetson + STM32 over CAN
SensorsRGB-D camera, IMU, joint encoders, tactile (later)
SoftwareUbuntu + ROS 2, LeRobot, MuJoCo / Isaac Lab

Height and DoF are not fixed up front. They will be set after checking actuator torque, body weight, power capacity and cost.

7VLA tasks

No walking at first: a fixed upper body and two arms manipulate objects. Data is collected with LeRobot to compare SmolVLA, ACT and Diffusion Policy.

TaskExample instructionMetrics
1 Grasp"Pick up the red block"Grasp success, position error
2 Pick & place"Put the blue block in the right box"Recognition, grasp, placement success
3 Bimanual"Hold the box with your left hand, take the item out with your right"Coordination success, contact stability
4 Recovery"Re-grasp the object that slipped"Failure detection, recovery success

8Vision-tactile world model

A latent state learned from vision, joint states and touch predicts action outcomes and failures, bringing my main research theme to the humanoid.

zt = E(ot),  ẑt+1 = F(zt, at)
et = ‖zt+1 − ẑt+1‖²

The error et is an anomaly feature, combined with task state and contact before calling a failure. Targets: slips, failed grasps and recovery.

9Cautions

  • Safety. Physical e-stop, joint torque and angle limits, temperature monitoring; walking tests on a support rig
  • Cost. Budget caps of ¥200–500k up to the upper body and ¥0.5–1M+ including legs; buy in stages
  • Licenses. Check each BHL repository before reusing its CAD or code
